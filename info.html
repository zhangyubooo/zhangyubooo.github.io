Info

I've always found it hard to define my interests through a single design discipline. Rather than focusing only on interfaces, products, or spaces, I'm drawn to how those experiences connect. For me, the object of design isn't necessarily a screen or an artifact — it can be the whole arc a person moves through across different touchpoints. That's what led me toward technology as a way of dissolving the boundaries between media, so that digital, physical, and spatial experiences behave as one continuous system.

I'm especially drawn to the new kinds of interaction that emerging technology makes possible. Rather than using technology for its own sake, what interests me is a sharper question: what experience does this new capability make possible that wasn't before? Does it actually make the relationship between people and a product, a space, or a system feel more natural? Studying Design and Product Management at CMU has pushed me to think about this from a fuller product perspective — not only whether an experience is compelling, but whether it meets a real user need, is technically feasible, and can become something genuinely valuable.

Next, I want to work on problems that still hold a lot of unknowns — especially ones sitting at the intersection of design, technology, and product. I enjoy working alongside engineers, designers, researchers, and product teams to take a vague idea and turn it into something real and experienceable. Whether it ends up as a digital product, a smart object, or an interactive space, I want to keep exploring how technology creates new behaviors, new experiences, and new product possibilities.
